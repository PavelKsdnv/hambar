Arable
working title

Factorio's pull, grown in real dirt: chains that gather → clean → transform → sell, where the market decides which link is worth running today.

You're a convict dropped on a dead world. Parole means proving the planet can feed itself — one viability tier at a time. The authority watches, buys your output, and decides when you've earned the next thing.

02

Systems & why we want them

Production

Five categories, not fifty crops

Grains, vegetables, fruits, herbs, and animals + their products. Each category has its own chain shape — grain mills, fruit ferments, herb extracts, dairy.
Why: keeps the variety and the distinct processing rhythms without drowning the player (or you) in barley-vs-wheat-vs-hops micro-variants.
Production

Product tiers per chain

Wheat → flour → bread. Milk → pasteurised → cheese. Each step is a choice to sell now or invest in the next transform.
Why: vertical depth inside one resource gives the Factorio "now automate the next thing" climb without needing a huge tech sprawl.
Economy

Reactive dynamic market

Prices float. You read what's valuable now and pivot — maybe with a small heads-up (a merchant's list, a shortage rumour) so you can react in time.
Why: turns "maximise output" into "make decisions." Skill is in reading and re-routing, not just building bigger. Kept reactive to stay snappy.
Progression

Proof-of-viability gating

You don't buy cows — you earn them. Sustain a grain operation and the authority sends livestock: "you can feed them now, prove you can handle them."
Why: diegetic unlocks. Each gate is the world saying "show me you can handle this complexity," which doubles as the narrative spine.
Narrative

Prisoner → parole arc

Exiled to prove a dead world habitable. Endgame is a sustainability threshold = your parole. Leave, or stay because you built something real.
Why: gives every gate, inspection, and quota a reason to exist. The market isn't abstract — it's them testing whether the colony is viable.
Pressure

Three-speed hazards

WEEDSconstant low drain — forces ongoing maintenance.
SPOILAGEtime-urgent — process before it degrades.
PESTScatastrophic but plannable-against.
Why: this is the biters' replacement. A rhythm of slow / urgent / spiky pressure means you're never just coasting, and difficulty stays tunable.
Sci-fi layer

Lost-civilisation tech

The planet had a prior species. Dig up their engineering — growth accelerants, water purifiers, soil-magic that Earth tools can't match. Found = works immediately.
Why: a second progression track beside Earth-tech, and a license for "magical" upgrades without breaking immersion. Instant-use keeps the find satisfying.
Sci-fi layer

Competency-gated, not map-gated

The alien gear sits on the map from the start, but stays locked until you've proven the relevant skill — sustained a harvest, raised livestock.
Why: stops a third-playthrough beeline to the best tool. Even veterans play the core loop before they shortcut. Replayability without removing the toy.
⏸ Back burner
Neighbouring farms / competitors. Tempting — rivals selling milk could crash your milk price (real supply & demand), and they'd move the market organically. But it drags in a whole simulation of their decision-making and risks the player feeling like they're fighting the system. Parked until the core loop is solid.
